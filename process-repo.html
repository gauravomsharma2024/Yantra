Standard Operating Procedure · Loan Origination
Generated from loan-origination@v1.3 · credit-policy@v1.4 · kyc-policy@v2.1 · 2026-05-12
1
Receive application Applications arrive via the loan-origination channel. Do not intervene unless the orchestrator agent assigns a case to your HITL queue.
2
Monitor the channel The agent handles steps 1–3 automatically — identity verify, bureau pull, AA fetch. Your work begins only when a case arrives in My Queue.
3
Review the agent's handoff note Read the reasoning, not just the recommendation. The evidence cards (left: agent data, right: source docs) are side-by-side. Do not redo the bureau pull — the agent's data is fresh.
4
Make your decision: Resume, Re-scan, or Decline Resume → you agree with the agent's assessment. Re-scan → you suspect a data error (agent re-runs steps 2–3). Decline → case does not meet policy.
5
Document your rationale — be specific Your rationale is written to team institutional memory. Future agents read it. "Kothrud → Hadapsar, 8 km intra-city, visual OCR confirmed" is useful. "Approved" is not.
6
Booking confirmation After your approval the agent completes the Finacle booking event. The loan ID appears in the channel within 60 seconds. No further action required from you.
Key thresholds
· Loan > ₹25 L → always UW queue, even if no flags
· CIBIL < 650 → CRO escalation (not UW)
· CIBIL < 620 → auto-decline, no override possible
· ≥ 2 amber flags → HITL required (§4.2)
· Addr mismatch ≤ 25 km → approvable with haircut (§8.1)

Frequently asked questions · ops staff
Q Why is the application stuck in my queue?
The agent paused because two or more amber flags were raised (credit-policy §4.2). It needs a human judgment call outside automatic approval bounds. The agent's reasoning and recommendation are in the handoff note — start there, not with the raw application.
Q Can I override a kernel-based auto-decline?
No. Kernel rules KR-LO-006 and below are deterministic by design. If CIBIL < 620, the process cannot proceed regardless of other factors. This is a hard RBI compliance boundary. Contact the credit risk team if you believe the rule needs revision — that goes through the policy amendment workflow, not a case override.
Q The Aadhaar address does not match. What do I do?
Check the distance. If ≤ 25 km (intra-city), you can approve under §8.1 with a standard haircut. Document the distance and your visual confirmation in your rationale. If > 25 km, escalate to CRO — this falls outside sr. underwriter authority.
Q What happens to my HITL rationale after I submit?
It is written to team-uw/hitl-log/ under your user ID, timestamped and version-pinned. It becomes part of team institutional memory — future agents read it when handling similar cases. It is also pinned in the decision log and is fully audit-visible.
Q The bureau score looks wrong. Can the agent re-run it?
Click "Re-scan" in your HITL queue. This re-runs steps 2–3 with fresh connector calls. Do not click Resume if you suspect a data error. The Re-scan path is specifically designed to handle stale or incorrect connector responses without creating a compliance gap.
Q The customer says their AA statement is missing an account. What do I do?
Ask the customer to check their AA consent status on their banking app. If they can add the missing account and refresh consent, click Re-scan — the agent will re-fetch. If they cannot, request alternative bank statements manually and use the manual-docs exception path documented in §7.2 of the SOP.

Common mistakes · from training data
Approving full amount on addr mismatch
§8.1 requires a haircut when Aadhaar addr ≠ application addr, even if the distance is within tolerance. 31% of trainees miss this on Scenario 1.
Declining on CIBIL 632 (thin file)
AA data is co-equal evidence under §8.3. Cooperative bank thin-file ≠ auto-decline. DSCR 1.6× overrides near-floor bureau. 44% wrong on Scenario 3.
Submitting vague HITL rationale
Generic entries ("approved") are flagged by audit review. Rationale must name the specific policy section and the specific evidence reviewed. Seen in 22% of submissions.
